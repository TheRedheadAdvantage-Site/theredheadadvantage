Why working hard is not always enough

Doing excellent work matters, but promotion decisions are also about visibility, evidence, and whether leaders can see you solving the problems of the next level.

Watch on TikTok

I used to think that if I did excellent work, the right people would notice. Sometimes they did, but doing good work and being seen as ready for a bigger role are two different things.

Most organizations don’t promote people simply because they’re busy, dependable, or overdue for recognition. They promote people when they believe that person can solve a problem at the next level.

That means your work has to be visible in the right way, and your manager needs to understand the decisions you made, the results they produced, and the kind of responsibility you’re ready to take on. If all they see is someone who quietly handles everything, they may value you enormously in your current role without imagining you in a different one.

This doesn’t mean you need to talk about yourself constantly, but be more deliberate in the work you’re doing, and who you’re getting that work in front of.

When you’re looking to move up, ask what the next role actually requires, find out who makes the decision and what evidence they look for, then make sure your work gives them that evidence.

A useful question to take into your next conversation with your manager is: “What would you need to see from me to feel confident recommending me for the next level?”

Listen carefully to the answer. If it’s specific, you have something to work with. If it stays vague after repeated conversations, that tells you something too.

Working hard still matters, but you deserve to know whether the work you’re doing is moving you toward the career you want.
