Money

Six figures does not automatically mean freedom

A high salary can create breathing room, but financial freedom depends on what the money makes possible after it comes in.

A higher salary can change your life. It can give you breathing room, choices, and the ability to help people you love. I would never pretend it doesn’t matter.

However, earning a lot and having financial freedom aren’t the same thing.

If every raise gets absorbed into a bigger mortgage, more payments, and a lifestyle that requires your income to stay exactly where it is, you can earn six figures and still feel unable to say no. The paycheck is large, but so are the obligations attached to it.

That’s why I care about what happens after the money comes in. How much of it do you keep? How long could you cover your essential expenses if your income changed? Are you building assets, or only funding a more expensive month?

These questions aren’t about living as cheaply as possible. I want people to enjoy what they’ve worked for, but I also want that work to buy them something lasting: the ability to leave a bad situation, take a thoughtful risk, or make a decision without immediate financial panic.

The best thing money ever bought me was the ability to say no. Not to everything, and not without consequences, but having more room to choose changed the way I thought about work.

A raise can help you build that room, but it won’t do it automatically. The real measure of progress is whether your money is making your life more secure and your choices more your own.
